# Gu Baojing Method Extraction

This note records a first-pass method extraction from Baojing Gu's public profile and representative public-facing papers. It is not yet a full bibliography crawl. Its goal is to capture reusable workflow patterns that can transfer to other environmental, agricultural, and sustainability topics.

## Core Interpretation

The recurring contribution is not just "nitrogen research." The reusable pattern is to treat one environmental variable as a system hub and then connect:

- environmental burden;
- production or ecosystem function;
- health, welfare, or SDG relevance;
- scenario and policy portfolios;
- boundary or target logic.

## Representative Paper to Pattern Map

### Science Advances

Safe-boundary framing for environmental pressure under ecological constraints.

Reusable pattern:

- dynamic safe operating space;
- changing environmental capacity over time;
- policy relevance through boundary tracking rather than only burden reporting.

### Nature Geoscience

Nitrogen waste, SDGs, and policy portfolios.

Reusable pattern:

- one burden linked to multiple development goals;
- technical measures compared with broader system portfolios;
- explicit co-benefit language with cost framing.

### Nature Sustainability

Elevated CO2 and cropland nitrogen-cycle coupling.

Reusable pattern:

- environmental change modifies nutrient-cycle efficiency;
- production and pollution must be interpreted together;
- future-condition framing changes the meaning of today's management conclusions.

### Nature Plants

Yield and N2O dual-outcome framing.

Reusable pattern:

- productivity and emissions evaluated together;
- trait or canopy structure treated as a management-relevant lever;
- co-benefit logic stronger than single-endpoint reporting.

### Nature Communications

Hidden-layer or hotspot identification.

Reusable pattern:

- dominant process layer can sit below the usual observation zone;
- system diagnosis improves when depth, pathway, or hidden compartment is made explicit;
- management recommendations depend on locating the true hotspot.

### One Earth

Global reduction portfolio framing.

Reusable pattern:

- compare sectoral and structural pathways rather than one intervention;
- ask what fraction of the target is reachable by technical options alone;
- keep system redesign in view.

## Why This Transfers Well

This method family can be reused for:

- carbon and methane;
- phosphorus and potassium;
- irrigation and water quality;
- air pollution and crop productivity;
- biodiversity and ecosystem stability;
- food-system pressure and food-security feedbacks;
- circular nutrient and waste-management planning.
