# Journal Figure Format Notes

This note records a few reusable figure-format lessons inspired by journals frequently appearing in the repository, including *The ISME Journal*, *Global Change Biology*, and *Nature* family titles.

## Core Takeaway

Before polishing style, decide which kind of graphic you are making:

- line art or statistical graphics;
- raster image or photograph;
- mixed figure with labels plus images;
- multi-panel figure;
- extended data or supplementary display.

## Nature Family

Useful figure-guide defaults from Nature's public figure guide:

- single-column width: `89 mm`
- double-column width: `183 mm`
- maximum figure height: `170 mm`
- text size should stay within `5-7 pt`
- use standard fonts such as `Arial` or `Helvetica`
- keep panel arrangement tight and efficient
- avoid decorative icons and avoid coloured text

Nature also expects figure legends to travel with the manuscript text at submission, and Extended Data figures should follow similar styling rules even though they are online-only.

## Wiley-Style Defaults

Wiley's public figure-preparation guidance is useful for journals such as *Global Change Biology*:

- line art: prefer `PDF`
- photographs or image-style figures: prefer `PNG` or `TIFF`
- line art: target `600 dpi`
- image figures: target `300 dpi`
- width often falls in the `80-180 mm` range
- one figure per file is preferred
- legends should be in a separate figure-legend section in the manuscript

## OUP / ISME-Style Practical Notes

Public author instructions for *The ISME Journal* emphasize:

- accessibility and readable contrast;
- captions that fully describe the image;
- careful handling of reused copyrighted figures;
- original research significance and strong visual communication.

For practical preparation, it is usually safe to use:

- `PDF` for line-based plots, networks, schematics, and vector-heavy panels;
- `TIFF` or `PNG` for image-heavy panels;
- `Arial`-style fonts and consistent panel lettering.

## Working Defaults For Your Own Papers

If you are preparing figures before submission:

- scatter, box, line, heatmap, network, ordination: start with `PDF`
- microscopy or photo-style images: export `TIFF` at `300 dpi`
- mixed panel figures: assemble cleanly, then keep a vector master and export final files to the journal-preferred format
- panel labels: `A`, `B`, `C` ...
- keep text readable at final size
- remove excess white space

## Why This Matters

Many rejections are not caused by figure file format alone. But poor figure readability creates an immediate signal of weak submission quality. Clear panel structure, standard fonts, correct sizes, and journal-appropriate export settings help the work feel publication-ready before a reviewer reads deeply.
